Will it change what you pay? Hinojosa's promise to freeze home electricity rates

On the campaign trail, Gov.-elect Gina Hinojosa (D) promised: "On Day One as governor, I'll freeze residential electricity rates and make corporations finally pay their fair share."

What she says she'll do:
• Declare a "high-cost emergency" within an hour of taking the oath on Jan. 19, she told the Texas Tribune. She said the same order would also suspend the state gas and diesel tax.
• Have the Public Utility Commission freeze residential rates until new laws make data centers and other high-load users pay for their own power.

Where prices stand: the average Texas home paid 15.91 cents per kilowatt-hour in August 2026, up from 15.40 cents a year earlier. The U.S. average was 18.16 cents. (U.S. EIA)

The open questions: Most Texans buy power from competing retail providers whose prices the state doesn't set, so how a freeze would work isn't clear yet. Lasting changes would need the Legislature, and Republicans still hold the Texas Senate.

How each side sees it:
Supporters say data centers are pushing up household bills and families need relief now.
Critics, including Nick Loris of the conservative group C3 Solutions in a Fox News story, say capping prices in a competitive market could discourage new power supply and will likely be challenged in court.

Promise status: not started. She takes office Jan. 19, 2027, and we'll track whether the freeze happens and holds up.
